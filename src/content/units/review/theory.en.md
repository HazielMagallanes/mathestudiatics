This unit revisits the tools used throughout the rest of the course:
operations with fractions, powers and roots, logarithms, linear equations and
intervals.

## Operations with fractions

To add or subtract fractions, find a **common denominator**: multiply the
numerator and denominator of each fraction by the missing factor.

$$\frac{a}{b} \pm \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot d \pm c \cdot b}{b \cdot d}$$

The result is **simplified** by dividing numerator and denominator by their
greatest common divisor. Multiplication works across, and division multiplies by
the reciprocal:

$$
\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot c}{b \cdot d}, \qquad
\frac{a}{b} : \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot d}{b \cdot c}
$$

## Powers and roots

A negative exponent inverts the base, and a fractional exponent is a root:

$$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}, \qquad a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a}$$

To **extract factors** from a root, write the radicand as a square (or cube)
times a remainder: for example $72 = 36 \cdot 2$, so $\sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2}$.

## Logarithms

A logarithm answers the question "which exponent of the base gives this
number?":

$$\log_b(a) = c \iff b^c = a$$

The decimal logarithm ($\log$) uses base $10$ and the natural logarithm ($\ln$)
uses base $e$. The logarithm of a proper fraction is negative, because it
corresponds to a negative exponent.

## Linear equations

A linear equation is solved by **isolating** the unknown: group the terms with
the variable on one side and the numbers on the other, then divide by the
coefficient. Always **check** the solution by substituting it back into the
original equation.

## Intervals

An interval is a set of real numbers between two endpoints. Endpoints are
included with a bracket ($\le$ or $\ge$) and excluded with a parenthesis ($<$
or $>$). Infinity is **never** included: it always takes a parenthesis.
